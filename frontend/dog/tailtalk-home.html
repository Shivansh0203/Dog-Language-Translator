Sound and body language, read together.

A bark on its own is ambiguous. The same sound can mean joy or fear, so Tailtalk weighs what it hears against what it sees.

  1. Hears the bark

    Pitch, rhythm and length are pulled from the live audio. Short high bursts, low steady warnings and thin whines each leave a different mark.

  2. Watches the body

    The camera follows tail height, ear position, posture and gaze, frame by frame. These are the cues a trainer reads by eye.

  3. Talks it through

    The two signals become one mood. Tailtalk explains it in the chat in plain words, with a suggestion for what to do next.
